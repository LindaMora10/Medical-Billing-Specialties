# MCG R-0056 Low Back Pain and Lumbar Spine Conditions — Referral Management

## Working Summary

**Purpose:** internal billing reference — the conservative-care and red-flag context behind facet interventions, including CPT 64772 (medial branch transection / rhizotomy), Dr. Timothy A. Luke, NPI 1437125846, Phoenix/Mesa AZ.
**Source:** MCG R-0056 (accessed via AZ Blue MCG portal, 09/22/2026). Proprietary MCG content — this is a paraphrased working summary, not a copy of the guideline. Pull the original from the portal for any appeal submission.
**Companions:** A-0218 Facet Neurotomy, A-0695 Facet Joint Injection.

---

## 1. Read this first — what R-0056 is and isn't

This is a **referral management** guideline. It addresses when a low back pain patient should be sent to a specialist, not whether a procedure is covered. Its Related CMS Coverage Guidance section reads **"None applicable"** — unlike A-0218 and A-0695, which both cite 42 CFR and the Medicare Benefit Policy Manual.

**What that means for 64772:** R-0056 will not be the guideline a reviewer denies under, and it won't get a claim paid. Its value is supporting: it describes the conservative-care and workup expectations that A-0218 and A-0695 assume have already happened, and it lists the red flags that must be excluded. Use it to build the record, not to argue coverage.

---

## 2. Emergent red flags — exclude and document

Referral for emergent evaluation is indicated for any of the following. Every one of these is also a reason **not** to proceed with a facet procedure, and A-0218 requires that other causes of spinal pain be ruled out.

- Cauda equina syndrome, known or suspected
- Bladder disturbance (e.g. acute urinary retention or incontinence)
- Bowel disturbance or flaccid anal sphincter
- Saddle anesthesia (perineal region)
- Motor deficits in both lower extremities (bilateral involvement)
- Spinal cord compression (e.g. myelopathy)
- Transverse myelitis
- Spinal fracture
- Spinal infection (e.g. vertebral osteomyelitis)
- Epidural abscess
- Spinal tumor
- Lytic bone lesion on spine imaging
- Recent significant trauma with concern for lumbar spine injury (e.g. fall from height, motor vehicle accident)

**Chart practice:** an explicit negative red-flag statement in the pre-procedure note directly supports the A-0218 exclusion criteria. It costs one sentence and closes a common denial argument.

## 3. Pain management referral

The guideline recognizes referral to pain management for evaluation or management of **chronic low back pain or prolonged narcotic usage**.

This is the entry point that places the patient in Dr. Luke's practice to begin with. It's a useful line to cite when a payer questions why a pain specialist is involved.

---

## 4. Conservative care — what counts

Throughout the guideline, failure of a trial of conservative care is defined as at least one of:

- Activity modification
- Analgesic or anti-inflammatory medication
- Exercise or physical therapy, as clinically indicated

Note this is a slightly different list than A-0218 and A-0695 use (exercise program / pharmacotherapy / physical therapy or spinal manipulation therapy). The overlap is substantial, and documentation that satisfies the facet guidelines will generally satisfy this one.

**Additional pathways the guideline recognizes:**
- *Physical medicine and rehabilitation referral* — education (e.g. back school), home exercise program, low back pain with or without radiculopathy, pain management
- *Physical therapy referral* — same list, plus rehabilitation needed after lumbar spinal stenosis surgery
- *Behavioral health referral* — cognitive behavioral therapy (e.g. to address misperceptions or poor coping skills), depression, psychosocial issues (e.g. job dissatisfaction), somatization

The behavioral health pathway matters because A-0695 lists cognitive behavioral therapy among nonoperative alternatives. If a payer argues conservative care was incomplete, a documented behavioral health referral or a reason it wasn't indicated strengthens the record.

## 5. Surgical referral criteria — the differential

Referral to neurosurgery or orthopedic spinal surgery is recognized for conditions including:

| Condition | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Lumbar herniated disc | Failed conservative care trial **and** nerve root compression consistent with clinical findings evident on imaging (e.g. MRI) |
| Lumbosacral spinal stenosis (with or without spondylolisthesis) | Failed conservative care trial, lumbar stenosis on imaging if the patient is a surgical candidate, **and** neurogenic claudication |
| Neurogenic claudication | Lumbar stenosis |
| Persistent low back pain | Despite a trial of conservative care |
| Significant pathology on imaging | Positive bone scan, lytic bone lesion in spine, pars defect (spondylolysis) in a patient age 20 or younger, or vertebral compression fracture with 50% or greater loss of height |
| Other | Localized bone pain; recent minor trauma in a patient older than 70 or with osteoporosis; scoliosis; skin stigmata overlying spinal defects; spine deformity in a child or adolescent |

**Why this matters for facet claims:** disc herniation with concordant radicular pain and spinal stenosis with neurogenic claudication are the two exclusions named explicitly in both A-0218 and A-0695. This section is where the guideline describes them. If imaging shows either, the facet procedure is not the indicated pathway, and a reviewer will say so.

## 6. Other specialty referrals recognized

- **Rheumatology** — ankylosing spondylitis, inflammatory back pain, limited range of motion, metabolic bone disease (e.g. osteoporosis, Paget disease), psoriatic spondylitis, reactive arthritis, spondyloarthropathy on spinal imaging
- **Neurology** — abnormal finding on spinal imaging (e.g. demyelination within spinal cord), demyelinating disease (e.g. multiple sclerosis), focal neurologic deficit (e.g. foot drop), equivocal neurologic examination
- **Infectious disease** — abnormal finding on spinal imaging (e.g. evidence of osteomyelitis), immunosuppression, recent genitourinary infection, spinal infection (e.g. diskitis, vertebral osteomyelitis), substance abuse (IV drug use with risk of osteomyelitis)
- **Oncology** — abnormal finding on spinal imaging (e.g. lytic lesion, positive bone scan), history or current diagnosis of cancer, immunosuppression
- **Endocrinology** — metabolic bone disease (e.g. osteoporosis, Paget disease)
- **Occupational medicine** — low back pain (e.g. need for work restrictions)

## 7. How to use this in a 64772 file

R-0056 is supporting documentation, not a coverage argument. Two practical uses:

**Before the procedure** — the pre-procedure note should affirmatively state:
- [ ] Red flags reviewed and absent (cauda equina, infection, fracture, tumor, progressive neuro deficit)
- [ ] No disc herniation with concordant radicular pain on imaging
- [ ] No spinal stenosis with neurogenic claudication
- [ ] Conservative care attempted and failed, with modality and duration named
- [ ] Referral pathway to pain management documented

**In an appeal** — if a payer argues the patient should have had surgery instead, or that conservative care was inadequate, R-0056 defines what the referral thresholds actually are. It can rebut an overreaching reviewer, though it carries no coverage weight of its own.
