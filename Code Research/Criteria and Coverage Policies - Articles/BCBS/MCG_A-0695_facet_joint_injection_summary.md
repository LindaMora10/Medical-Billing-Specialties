# MCG A-0695 Facet Joint Injection — Working Summary

**Purpose:** internal billing reference — the diagnostic-block prerequisite for facet neurotomy, including CPT 64772 (medial branch transection / rhizotomy), Dr. Timothy A. Luke, NPI 1437125846, Phoenix/Mesa AZ.
**Source:** MCG A-0695 (accessed via AZ Blue MCG portal, 09/22/2026). Proprietary MCG content — this is a paraphrased working summary, not a copy of the guideline. Pull the original from the portal for any appeal submission.
**Companion guideline:** A-0218 Facet Neurotomy.

---

## 1. The core point

Facet joint injection is supported as a **diagnostic** tool, not as a treatment. The stated goal is to diagnose facet joint pain in order to determine whether a patient is a candidate for facet neurotomy. Diagnostic use carries the guideline's strongest recommendation grade (RG A1 — at least moderate certainty of at least moderate net benefit). Therapeutic use is graded RG B (evidence insufficient, conflicting, or poor).

**Billing implication:** blocks billed to establish neurotomy candidacy are on solid ground. Blocks billed as ongoing pain treatment are not.

---

## 2. Approval criteria (all must be met)

**A diagnostic block is needed to confirm the facet joint as the pain source** — at least one of:
- Initial diagnostic block (dual diagnostic blocks are required to diagnose facet pain)
- Second confirmatory block, where documentation shows the first block produced 80% or greater relief of the primary (index) pain and the duration of relief was consistent with the agent used

**The patient is a candidate for facet neurotomy** — all of:
- Chronic spinal pain, 3+ months' duration, cervical (e.g. post-whiplash) or lumbar
- Failure of nonoperative management — at least one of: exercise program, pharmacotherapy, physical therapy or spinal manipulation therapy
- Imaging and physical exam have ruled out other causes (disc herniation with concordant radicular pain, spinal stenosis with neurogenic claudication, fracture, tumor)

**Other**
- No coagulopathy
- No current infection

---

## 3. The controlled diagnostic testing protocol

This is the sequence the guideline describes, and it's what the documentation needs to show:

1. Inject a **short-acting** anesthetic (e.g. lidocaine)
2. Patients with at least **80% short-term pain reduction** from baseline proceed to step 3
3. Inject a **longer-acting** agent (bupivacaine)
4. Patients with at least **80% pain reduction** from baseline after the longer-acting agent are considered candidates for facet neurotomy

**Medial branch block vs. intra-articular injection:** MBB is associated with better prediction of neurotomy success. Intra-articular injection is more difficult to perform and may have lower prognostic power. Use MBB.

---

## 4. Why dual blocks are required — false positive rates

| Region | Evidence base | False-positive rate |
|---|---|---|
| Cervical | 10 studies, 1,192 patients (75%+ relief criterion) | 27% to 63% |
| Lumbar | 14 studies, 2,804 patients (75%+ relief criterion) | 17% to 49% |
| Thoracic | 3 studies, 183 patients (80%+ relief criterion) | 42% to 58% |

Single-block diagnosis is unreliable at these rates, which is the reason for the dual-block requirement.

**Note on the threshold:** the guideline records a multispecialty consensus recommending 50% or greater reduction as defining a positive block, while acknowledging the exact figure remains uncertain. MCG's own criteria and the Noridian LCD both use **80%**. Document to 80%.

---

## 5. What is NOT supported

**Therapeutic intra-articular facet injection** — evidence insufficient, conflicting, or poor (RG B). For thoracic intra-articular injection specifically, a systematic review found no available literature investigating efficacy. Consensus guidelines recommend against routine use, while noting a possible role for patients with contraindications to radiofrequency ablation.

**Therapeutic medial branch nerve block** — evidence insufficient, conflicting, or poor (RG B). Consensus guidelines likewise recommend against routine use, with the same limited exception.

**Also noted:** one evidence-based clinical practice guideline cited here strongly recommends against both therapeutic facet corticosteroid injections and facet neurotomy for chronic cervical and lumbar pain, citing weak supporting evidence and poorly reported patient-important outcomes. The same citation appears in A-0218. Expect a payer reviewer to be aware of it.

## 6. Hierarchy note

Identical to A-0218: the guideline supplements but does not replace, modify, or supersede Medicare regulations, NCDs, or LCDs. It cites 42 CFR 419.22 and 42 CFR 422.101 and the Medicare Benefit Policy Manual chapters 14, 15, and 16.

Order of authority:
1. NCD — none for facet interventions
2. LCD/LCA — Noridian **L38801 / A58403** (Arizona, effective 04/16/2026; previously L38803 / A58405)
3. Plan's own medical policy
4. MCG A-0695 / A-0218

## 7. Pre-procedure documentation checklist

Before any 64772 claim goes out, the chart needs the block history this guideline requires:

- [ ] Two diagnostic blocks performed, fluoroscopically guided
- [ ] Medial branch blocks (not intra-articular) — better prognostic value
- [ ] First block: short-acting agent, 80%+ relief of index pain documented
- [ ] Second block: longer-acting agent, 80%+ relief documented
- [ ] Duration of relief consistent with the agent used, noted in the record
- [ ] Blocks at least 2 weeks apart (LCD requirement)
- [ ] 3+ months chronic cervical or lumbar pain
- [ ] Conservative care failure documented
- [ ] Imaging ruling out other causes
- [ ] No coagulopathy, no active infection
- [ ] Blocks documented as diagnostic, not therapeutic

**Watch for:** if the blocks were performed or documented as therapeutic, the neurotomy loses its diagnostic foundation and both the blocks and the neurotomy become vulnerable. The framing in the op note and the indication on the claim both matter.

---

## 8. Relevance to CPT 64772

A-0695 governs the blocks that come before the procedure, so it applies cleanly regardless of which technique is used for the denervation itself. Unlike A-0218, there's no method mismatch here — the diagnostic pathway is the same whether the subsequent denervation is radiofrequency or transection.

That makes this the more defensible half of the case. The block documentation can fully satisfy A-0695 even while the neurotomy itself falls outside A-0218's described technique. Build the record accordingly: a clean, complete dual-block history removes one line of denial and isolates the dispute to the method question.
