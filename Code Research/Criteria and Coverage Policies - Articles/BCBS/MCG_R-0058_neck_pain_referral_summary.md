# MCG R-0058 Neck Pain and Cervical Spine Conditions — Referral Management

## Working Summary

**Purpose:** internal billing reference — the conservative-care and red-flag context behind cervical facet interventions, including CPT 64772 (medial branch transection / rhizotomy), Dr. Timothy A. Luke, NPI 1437125846, Phoenix/Mesa AZ.
**Source:** MCG R-0058 (accessed via AZ Blue MCG portal, 09/22/2026). Proprietary MCG content — this is a paraphrased working summary, not a copy of the guideline. Pull the original from the portal for any appeal submission.
**Companions:** A-0218 Facet Neurotomy, A-0695 Facet Joint Injection, R-0056 (lumbar equivalent).

## 1. Same status as R-0056

This is a **referral management** guideline, and its Related CMS Coverage Guidance section reads **"None applicable."** It does not establish coverage for any procedure and won't be the basis of a denial or an approval.

Use it the same way as R-0056: to build the pre-procedure record and to rebut a reviewer who claims the patient should have gone a different route.

---

## 2. Emergent red flags — exclude and document

Referral for emergent evaluation is indicated for any of the following:

- Bladder or bowel dysfunction
- Cardiac etiology (e.g. angina, aortic dissection)
- Pathologic reflexes suggestive of spinal cord compression (myelopathy) — positive Babinski sign, clonus, positive Hoffmann sign, or hyperreflexia in the upper extremities
- Recent significant trauma with concern for cervical spine injury (e.g. fall from height, motor vehicle accident)
- Spinal infection
- Spinal pathology on imaging — cervical spinal cord compression (e.g. spondylotic myelopathy), epidural abscess, lytic bone lesion, or spinal fracture
- Subluxation of the cervical spine (e.g. with rheumatoid arthritis)

**Note the cardiac entry.** Neck pain has a cardiac differential that the lumbar guideline has no counterpart to. Worth a line in the note.

**Chart practice:** document the myelopathy exam explicitly — Hoffmann, Babinski, clonus, hyperreflexia — as negative. A-0218 requires other causes of spinal pain be ruled out, and for cervical levels this is the exam that does it.

---

## 3. Cervical conservative care — the fuller list

For persistent neck pain or cervical radiculopathy, failure of a trial of conservative care is defined as at least one of:

- Activity modification
- Analgesics or anti-inflammatory medication
- Instruction on home exercise program
- **Muscle relaxants**
- Physical therapy
- Spinal manipulation therapy, as clinically indicated

This list is longer than the lumbar version in R-0056 and longer than the list in A-0218. Muscle relaxants and formal home exercise instruction are specific to it.

**Practical point:** document whichever of these were tried, with duration. A-0218 needs only one modality, but a fuller record is harder to argue with.

---

## 4. Pain management referral

The guideline recognizes referral to pain management for evaluation or management of **chronic neck pain or prolonged narcotic use** — the cervical parallel to the lumbar entry in R-0056.

---

## 5. Cervicogenic headache — important cross-reference

R-0058 routes **cervicogenic headaches** to a **neurology** referral.

This matters because A-0218 grades the evidence for facet neurotomy in cervicogenic headache as insufficient, conflicting, or poor, and does not support it. Taken together, the two guidelines point the same direction: if the presentation is headache rather than axial neck pain, the facet route is not supported and neurology is the recognized pathway.

**Do not** list cervicogenic headache as the indication on a 64772 claim. Axial neck pain only.

---

## 6. Surgical referral criteria

Referral to neurosurgery or orthopedic spinal surgery is recognized for:

| Category | Findings |
|---|---|
| Neurologic | Progressive neurologic deficit; spastic gait or difficulty walking; wasting of intrinsic hand musculature; weakness involving more than one muscle group |
| Autonomic | Bladder disturbance (e.g. acute urinary retention or incontinence); bowel disturbance |
| Structural | Neck deformity; subluxation of cervical spine (e.g. with rheumatoid arthritis); history of neck surgery with hardware instrumentation |
| Pain | Persistent neck pain or symptoms of cervical radiculopathy despite a trial of conservative care |
| Other | Localized neck tenderness; recent trauma |

**Relevance to facet claims:** cervical radiculopathy is the cervical analogue of the exclusion A-0218 names for the lumbar spine. If radicular symptoms are present and concordant with imaging, the facet pathway is not the indicated route and a reviewer will redirect.

---

## 7. Other specialty referrals recognized

- **Neurology** — abnormal imaging finding (e.g. demyelination within cervical spinal cord), bladder or bowel disturbance, cervical radiculopathy, cervicogenic headaches, demyelinating disease (e.g. multiple sclerosis), electrodiagnostic testing needed, interpretation of imaging or other diagnostic testing, Lhermitte sign (tingling with neck flexion), loss of sensation in more than one dermatome, motor deficits in upper or lower extremities, equivocal neurologic examination, spastic gait or difficulty walking, wasting of intrinsic hand musculature, weakness involving more than one muscle group
- **Rheumatology** — abnormal imaging finding (e.g. demyelination within cervical spinal cord), inflammatory causes of neck pain (e.g. rheumatoid arthritis, seronegative spondyloarthropathies), unclear diagnosis
- **Oncology** — significant pathology on imaging tests (e.g. lytic bone lesion, tumor, metastasis)
- **Physical medicine and rehabilitation** — electrodiagnostic testing needed, home exercise program, pain management, reducing mechanical stress on the cervical spine or awkward positions
- **Physical therapy** — home exercise program, neck pain with or without radiculopathy, pain management
- **Speech therapy** — e.g. for dysphagia or dysphonia following anterior cervical spine surgery

## 8. How to use this in a 64772 file

**Before a cervical procedure** — the pre-procedure note should state:

- [ ] Myelopathy exam negative (Hoffmann, Babinski, clonus, hyperreflexia)
- [ ] No bladder or bowel dysfunction
- [ ] Cardiac etiology considered and excluded where the presentation warranted it
- [ ] No cervical radiculopathy concordant with imaging
- [ ] No cervical cord compression, fracture, infection, or lytic lesion on imaging
- [ ] No cervical subluxation
- [ ] Conservative care attempted and failed, modality and duration named
- [ ] Indication documented as **axial** neck pain, not cervicogenic headache
- [ ] 3+ months' duration

**Cervical-specific reminders from the companion guidelines:**
- A-0695 records a cervical diagnostic block false-positive rate of 27% to 63% — the highest of the three regions, which is why dual blocks matter most here
- A-0218 covers cervical and lumbar only; thoracic is not supported
- Both require 80% relief from each of two blocks
